Three Workers Missing Beneath the Aorta

A night shift failed to return from the municipal pressure tunnels. The authorities insist that no public danger exists.

By I. VellerFrom our own correspondentsHeartwell edition

The lamps were still burning

Three municipal workers were reported missing after a maintenance party entered the old pressure tunnels shortly before the second bell on Tuesday. Their names have been withheld pending notification of their families. The foreman, who returned alone, was taken to the Senatorium for observation.

The official report describes a partial collapse in an abandoned service passage. Yet the lamps recovered at the scene were still lit, their glass unbroken and their wicks newly trimmed. A length of wet black ribbon was found tied around the nearest handrail.

Residents of the Aorta district have complained of a low vibration beneath the cobbles. It is strongest after midnight and seems to follow the route of the disused railway. The tram authority denies that any underground service is operating.

The tunnel was empty when we went in. It was not empty when we came out.

A railway that was never removed

The Gazette spoke with a retired signalman who remembers a second railway, built before the city walls were completed. Its plans were withdrawn after an accident in the northern cutting. He claims that the line was never dismantled, merely sealed behind the brickwork.

A constable stationed near the old pump house told our reporter that the missing men had been seen entering the tunnel with a fourth figure. The constable declined to describe the person and was transferred before his statement could be recorded.

The families have asked the city to continue the search. The municipal office has instead announced a temporary restriction on access to all service passages. Citizens are reminded that entering the tunnels is a punishable offence.

From the editor's desk

The families have established a collection at the Copper Saint public house. Contributions may be made anonymously. No official relief fund has been announced.
