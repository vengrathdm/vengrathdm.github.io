Monday, 18 October 1926PRICE: TWO PENCE · ALL DISTRICTSVOL. VII

Seven Princes, One City — and No Agreement

An emergency convocation was called last night as the foundries fell silent and the eastern quarter prepared for another night beneath curfew.

By E. MarrowFrom our own correspondentsHeartwell edition

The doors remained closed until dawn

The bells of the Assembly Hall began at eleven, though no public summons had been posted. By midnight, seven motorcars had passed through the iron gates, each bearing the colours of a different princely house. The city was asked to remain indoors. The city, as usual, did not listen.

A short communiqué released at first light described the meeting as a routine review of municipal security. Workers leaving the Aorta found the gates closed and the tramlines empty. Several witnesses reported a column of smoke rising from the council tower, but the watchmen declined to comment.

Inside the foundries, the furnaces have been cold for three nights. The Guild of Engineers attributes the stoppage to a shortage of refined coal. The unions point to the recent arrests. Neither explanation accounts for the heavy wagons seen entering the lower vaults.

A city may survive a disagreement between its rulers. It seldom survives the secret they agree to keep.

A matter omitted from the minutes

A source close to the eastern household, speaking on condition of anonymity, suggested that the Princes were not called together to discuss the strike. The subject, our correspondent was told, concerned a sealed room beneath the old observatory and a name absent from the public registers.

The Gazette has been unable to verify this account. We can confirm only that the observatory has been closed to visitors, that its clocks have stopped, and that the constabulary has established a second perimeter around the hill.

For the present, citizens are advised to carry their papers, avoid the lower passages after dusk, and disregard rumours concerning the seventh bell. The municipal office assures us that there is no cause for alarm.

From the editor's desk

The official minutes have not yet been made available for public inspection. Our request for a copy was returned unopened, bearing a seal not used by any recognised department.
