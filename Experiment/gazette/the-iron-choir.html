Midnight Song Reported in the Lower Foundry

Residents describe a chorus beneath the factory floor. The company says the building has been empty for eleven years.

By T. SallowFrom our own correspondentsHeartwell edition

A factory without a power supply

For eleven years, the Lower Foundry has stood behind a wall of corrugated iron, its windows bricked and its doors chained. On Wednesday night, residents of three adjoining streets reported hearing singing from beneath the floor. The sound began at twelve minutes past midnight and continued for precisely seven minutes.

A local watchmaker recorded the final minute on a wax cylinder. The recording contains a low mechanical rhythm, a chorus of voices, and a series of names spoken in unison. The first six are difficult to distinguish. The seventh is perfectly clear.

The name belongs to a councillor who was photographed at a public reception on the same evening. The councillor's office has described the recording as a forgery and requested that the cylinder be destroyed.

The machines are silent. The building is not.

The old furnace sequence

The Gazette has listened to the recording twice. It is not possible to determine how many voices are present. The rhythm resembles the old furnace sequence used to test pressure valves, although the plant has been disconnected from the municipal grid for more than a decade.

A former engineer believes the sound could travel through the abandoned ventilation shafts. He also notes that the shafts were sealed with poured concrete, a procedure that would make the reported volume impossible.

The city has issued no new safety notice. Residents are advised to keep windows closed after midnight and to report any further singing to the district watch.

From the editor's desk

The wax cylinder has been deposited with a private conservator. The Gazette has been advised not to publish the names recorded on it until the matter has been investigated.
