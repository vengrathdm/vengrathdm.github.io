A City Guide for the Curious and the Careful

Restaurants, theatres, public houses and the smaller places one ought not to find without an introduction.

By The Gazette city deskFrom our own correspondentsHeartwell edition

Where the visitor should begin

The winter visitor will find Heartwell a city of fine contradictions. Its avenues are broad, its stonework severe, and its public squares are often empty even on market days. The visitor should begin in the Crown district, where the principal hotels offer warm rooms and a respectable view of the river.

Those seeking entertainment may prefer the Aorta, where the theatres remain open late and the music halls rarely ask a second question. The old quarter offers quieter pleasures: antiquarian bookshops, private reading rooms and a modest collection of curiosities in the former customs house.

The city's trams are reliable before dusk. After that hour, routes may change without notice. Visitors are advised to keep a printed timetable and to ask a uniformed official before boarding any carriage marked with a white lantern.

Keep a printed map. In Heartwell, the streets remember a route long after the signposts forget it.

An evening's recommendations

A word about the public houses. The Copper Saint serves an excellent dark ale, though the proprietor has an unfortunate habit of closing the doors when strangers enter. The Lantern Room is more welcoming but should be avoided on Thursdays, when the upstairs rooms are reserved.

The municipal gardens are open until the second bell. The cemetery gates are not. The river walk is beautiful in clear weather and treacherous when the fog settles. A local guide may be hired from the steps of the central station, but references should be requested before payment.

Heartwell rewards the curious, provided they remember that not every door is intended to open.
