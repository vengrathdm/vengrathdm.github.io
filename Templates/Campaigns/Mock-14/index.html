The Salted
Coast Accord

A trade war, a drowned god, and five people who owe each other nothing — yet.

Three free ports once split the coastal trade evenly under a century-old accord. That accord died with the Merchant Prince last spring, and the ports have spent the season arming, bribing, and quietly dredging the harbor for something the treaty was written to keep buried. This journal tracks the world, the table, and the record of what's actually happened — replace anything below with your own.

THE SALTED COASTENTRY NO. 002 — WORLD

The three ports

Everything downstream of the plot starts here — who holds power, and what they're afraid of losing.

NORTH HARBOR

Cindral

Old money, older grudges. The Cindral shipping houses funded the original Accord and have the most to lose now that it's gone. Their navy is the coast's largest — and its most idle.

SOUTH HARBOR

Thessy's Landing

Built by refugees two generations ago, now the coast's manufacturing heart. Thessy's has the labor and the grievances; it lacks ships of its own and resents needing Cindral's.

MID HARBOR

Farrow Cay

A free port in name, a smugglers' clearinghouse in practice. Farrow Cay profits from the other two fighting and has quietly funded both sides' worst decisions.
